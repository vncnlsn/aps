What APS does

Services built around project risk, property rights, and trust.

Technical guide

Ownership research & title verification

Before a field crew moves a line, stakes a route, or begins work on private property, the legal and practical reality of that land needs to be understood. APS researches the records that shape access: who owns the surface, who owns the minerals, whether easements already exist, whether there are title issues, and whether additional stakeholders must be contacted.

Good research reduces delays, avoids miscommunication, and helps ensure compensation and agreements are directed to the proper parties.

•

Surface ownership

Identify the property owner or owners responsible for access decisions.

•

Mineral ownership

Determine mineral interests and related parties when the project requires it.

•

Title curative

Resolve ownership questions and missing links that can otherwise hold up a project.

Why it matters

Bad information creates bad outcomes.

If ownership is wrong, the wrong person may be contacted. If title is incomplete, agreements may not reflect reality. If easements or prior rights are missed, the project may face avoidable conflict.

APS exists to reduce those risks before they become expensive problems.

1

Identify the proper stakeholders

Get the project in front of the right people early.

2

Reduce project uncertainty

Know what rights exist before field work begins.

3

Prevent costly delays

Good research helps keep the schedule intact.

Field relationships

Landowner relations done with clarity and restraint.

The right tone matters. Landowners need to understand what is happening, why it matters, how long it will take, and what protections are in place. APS focuses on respectful communication and accurate expectations from the first conversation through project closeout.

Why clients value it

Trust on the ground keeps the project moving.

Projects move faster when landowners feel informed rather than pressured. APS helps create that environment by handling outreach carefully, documenting agreements properly, and staying available when questions come up during the life of a project.

The result is a better experience for landowners and fewer surprises for the project team.
